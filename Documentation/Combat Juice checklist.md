# Combat Juice Checklist

**Use this as a pass/fail list.**  
If too many boxes are unchecked, combat will feel flat even if systems are deep.

**Note:** No heavy animations required. Focus on iconography, timers, pulses, color shifts, and minor motion.

1. Action Confirmation (Did the game hear me?)

Every card play must instantly answer:

“Yes, that happened.”

Checklist

☐ Card snaps or compresses slightly on play

☐ Mana visibly ticks down before effects resolve

☐ Sound or pitch shift unique to card type (Attack / Skill / Guard)

☐ Brief screen-space text or icon flash (“Crumble +2”, “Shock!”)

☐ Enemy intent icons react (shake / dim / update)

Rule:

If the player can blink and miss confirmation, it’s not enough.

## 2. Impact Readability (What changed?)

The board must clearly show state change, not just numbers.

### Checklist
- [ ] Status stacks visibly increment (count + pulse)
- [ ] Threat countdowns update with emphasis
- [ ] Affected enemies get a 0.2–0.3s glow or outline
- [ ] Buffs/debuffs float from source to target
- [ ] Board quiets for ~100ms after resolution (micro-pause)

**Rule:** Damage without state change is forgettable.

## 3. Directionality (Where did power go?)

Even without animations, motion implies causality.

### Checklist
- [ ] Effects move from card → target
- [ ] AoE ripples outward, not instant pop
- [ ] Chain effects show hop indicators
- [ ] Resource gains flow back to player UI
- [ ] Consumed stacks collapse inward visually

**Rule:** The player should see where the power traveled.

4. Escalation Feedback (Am I building something?)

Your game has stacks, charges, preparation — show momentum.

Checklist

☐ Stacks feel heavier at higher counts (bigger pulse / glow)

☐ Thresholds visually hinted (cracks, sparks, warning color)

☐ “Almost there” states look different from empty

☐ Release effects are louder / brighter than buildup

☐ UI subtly anticipates release (shimmer / tension)

Rule:

Build-up must feel like a promise.

5. Threat Presence (What should I fear?)

Enemies must feel dangerous before they act.

Checklist

☐ Threat icons are always visible

☐ Charging enemies visually “tick”

☐ Terminal threats override other visuals

☐ Suppression visibly distorts UI elements

☐ Ignoring a threat makes the board uglier

Rule:

Fear comes from clarity, not surprise.

## 6. Decision Weight (Did my choice matter?)

Players must feel divergence.

### Checklist
- [ ] Different card orders visibly differ
- [ ] Cancelled / interrupted threats react distinctly
- [ ] Overkill produces excess feedback (shatter, overflow)
- [ ] Wasted effects look sad (fizzle, dim)
- [ ] Perfect answers look clean and satisfying

**Rule:** Optimal play should look beautiful.

7. Turn Transitions (Punctuation matters)

Turns are sentences. End them clearly.

Checklist

☐ Turn start has a subtle “breath in”

☐ Enemy intent refresh is noticeable

☐ End turn locks input briefly

☐ Delayed effects announce themselves

☐ Wave transitions reset visual noise

Rule:

Silence between actions is part of the music.

## 8. Resource Emotion Mapping

Every resource should feel different.

### Checklist
- [ ] Mana feels liquid (flow, drain)
- [ ] Aggression feels sharp (spikes, red flashes)
- [ ] Focus feels clean (blue, smooth motion)
- [ ] Potential feels unstable (flicker, distortion)
- [ ] Preparation feels tense (tightening rings)

**Rule:** Resources are characters, not numbers.

9. Micro-Reward Frequency

Players need frequent “yes” moments.

Checklist

☐ Minor procs have minor feedback

☐ Big synergies have stacked feedback

☐ Rare triggers are unmistakable

☐ Combos chain feedback naturally

☐ Non-damage plays still feel rewarding

Rule:

If a clever play is quiet, it didn’t land.

10. Failure Feedback (This is critical)

Losing must teach, not frustrate.

Checklist

☐ Cause of damage is highlighted

☐ Final blow references threat vocabulary

☐ Post-combat summary shows “missed answers”

☐ Last turn is readable in hindsight

☐ Failure looks intentional, not random

Rule:

“I see why I died” is a win.

The Solo-Dev Rule of Thumb

If you only do 5 things, do these:

Stack pulses

Threat countdown ticks

Resource flow motion

Micro-pauses after actions

Strong sound design per card type

That alone can double perceived fun.

Final Reframe (Important)

You are not making a boring game.

You are making a systems-rich game that is under-communicating.

Fix communication — not mechanics — and combat will wake up.
